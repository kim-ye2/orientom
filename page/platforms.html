Many paths to quantum. One software layer.

Hardware-agnostic by design — always the right machine for the job.

There is no single way to build a quantum computer. Several hardware technologies are advancing in parallel,
each with its own strengths in speed, accuracy and scalability
— and no one yet knows which will prevail.

ORIENTOM is hardware-agnostic. Our software is designed to work across all major quantum platforms,
so our customers are never tied to a single technology and can always use the best machine for each problem.

Superconducting: Fast, scalable circuits at near absolute zero.

Microchip-based qubits with fast gate speeds and mature fabrication.

Superconducting qubits are tiny electrical circuits cooled close to absolute zero, where current flows without resistance.
With fast gate operations and semiconductor-style fabrication, it is one of the most widely adopted approaches today.
The road ahead focuses on scaling qubit counts and achieving practical quantum error correction.

Ion Trap: Nature's most precise qubits.

Individual ions controlled by lasers for industry-leading fidelity.

Trapped-ion quantum computers use individual charged atoms suspended by electromagnetic fields and controlled with lasers.
Because every ion is naturally identical, they offer exceptionally high gate fidelity, long coherence times and all-to-all qubit connectivity.
Scaling to larger, modular systems is the next frontier.

Neutral Atom: Thousands of atoms, arranged by light.

Flexible atom arrays held by optical tweezers for rapid scaling.

Neutral-atom systems arrange hundreds to thousands of uncharged atoms in programmable arrays using tightly focused laser beams,
known as optical tweezers. Their flexible geometry and rapid scalability make them strong candidates for large-scale quantum computing and
analog quantum simulation, including optimization problems.

Photonics: Computing at the speed of light.

Qubits made of light, built for networking and scale.

Photonic quantum computers encode information in particles of light.
They can operate with fewer cryogenic requirements and integrate naturally with optical-fiber networks, making them attractive for scalable and networked quantum computing. Advances in integrated photonic chips are rapidly bringing this vision closer.

Simulator: Quantum computing on classical hardware.

CPU / GPU-based emulation for
algorithm development and verification.

Quantum simulators reproduce quantum computations on classical CPUs and GPUs.
They are essential for developing, testing and teaching quantum
algorithms today, and serve as a trusted reference for verifying results from
real quantum devices.

ORIENTOM's Titanium-Q brings high-performance
quantum simulation to every developer.
